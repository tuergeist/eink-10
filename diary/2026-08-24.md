# 2026-08-24 — Grafana renderer

The Inkplate 10 now shows the Landstimme dashboard "Bestand & Kumuliert",
refreshed hourly. New component `renderer/`: a CronJob that queries
Grafana and pushes a PNG to the existing eink service.

## Why not the image renderer plugin

The obvious route — `GET /render/d/<uid>` — needs the
`grafana-image-renderer` plugin. The monitoring Grafana (12.3.0) does not
have it; `/render/...` answers 302 to the login page and the installed
plugin list is Infinity plus the Explore apps. Adding it means a
permanent Chromium pod in the shared monitoring namespace and a Grafana
restart.

That was worth avoiding on its own, but the output would also have been
worse. Eight gray levels do not carry Grafana's thin coloured series
lines or its ~11 px axis labels; both dither into noise at reading
distance.

So the renderer queries `/api/ds/query` with a Viewer service account and
draws the numbers itself. One call covers all seven panels — Grafana
answers any number of queries keyed by `refId`, which also means every
figure on the image comes from the same instant rather than seven
snapshots seconds apart.

## What generalises and what does not

The layout is not hardcoded to this dashboard. `gridPos` — Grafana's own
24-column grid — is scaled onto the panel area, so a panel moved in
Grafana moves on the paper. Panel types outside `timeseries`/`graph`/
`barchart` and `stat`/`gauge` render as a labelled placeholder rather
than vanishing silently.

What does not generalise: the drawing itself. Big figures, thick curves,
series distinguished by dash pattern instead of colour. That is a
deliberate deviation from what Grafana shows, and it is the reason the
result is legible at all.

## The property that mattered most

Identical numbers must produce byte-identical PNGs. The service hashes
the image and the firmware only redraws when the hash changed; a new hash
costs ~2 s of flashing and a slice of panel lifetime. Two consecutive
runs against production returned the same hash (`1022df94...`).

That measurement was too weak, and the first cluster run showed it: a
different hash minutes later. The dashboard range is `now-30d` and slides
continuously, so every data point drifts along the time axis — 0.69 px per
hour on a 500 px plot. Two runs seconds apart agree because the drift is
sub-pixel; two runs an hour apart do not. The hourly cron would have burnt
a panel refresh every single time.

Fixed by snapping the *axis* onto whole days (`panels.snap_range`) while
leaving the *query* range exact, so what Grafana returns is unchanged. The
axis now stands still for 24 hours. Short dashboard ranges get a finer
grid — a six-hour dashboard snapped to days would collapse to nothing.

The lesson is about the measurement, not the code: two runs seconds apart
cannot distinguish "stable" from "drifting slowly". The test that catches
it renders the same data an hour apart.

That is also why there is no timestamp on the image. The firmware already
overlays `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` when a new image arrives
(`EINK_OVERLAY_CLOCK=true`), and a clock drawn into the PNG would force a
refresh every hour regardless of the data. Only the date range in the
header varies, and that at most once a day.

Two corners stay empty for the same reason the clock exists: the firmware
paints a white box under its clock (bottom right) and under the
low-battery symbol (top right). `CLOCK_ZONE` and `BATTERY_ZONE` in
`draw.py` come from `firmware/src/main.cpp`, and two tests assert those
regions are untouched.

## Verified

- Container run against production Grafana: 1200×825, exactly the eight
  panel levels, no other gray value.
- Two pushes in a row, same hash — no wasted refresh.
- Service log shows `GET /c/inkplate10/config.json` followed by
  `GET /c/inkplate10/dashboard.png`: the board picked up the change and
  downloaded the image.
- 41 tests.

Serial monitoring on `/dev/cu.usbserial-10` produced nothing across two
attempts (plain `cat` and a termios reader at 115200). Not pursued — the
service log proves the board is awake and fetching, which is the question
that mattered.
